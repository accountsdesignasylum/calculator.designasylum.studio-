How the math works. Your costs and your profit target together make the money the month has to produce, and each project has to carry an equal share of it, so the floor is costs plus profit divided by the number of projects. Quote below the floor and the other projects have to cover the gap, because the costs do not shrink just because one client paid less.

If you set profit as a percentage, it is a share of what you bill, not a markup on your costs. So your costs have to fit inside the rest, which means total billing is costs divided by 100% minus that share. At 30% the costs take up 70% of what you bill, and that is why the percentage cannot reach 100.

One caveat. This assumes every project is the same size, which is rarely true. Use the floor as your walk-away number in a negotiation, and if one project comes in under it, know exactly how much the others now have to carry.
